Nagi v3: five concepts

The same evening on every board, with invented projects and accounts: three agents running under two projects, one of them asking a question; Codex 1 empty until Monday; Claude 1 with three windows; one Codex read that failed at 18:35; three connected sources. Each panel is 400 px wide, night and day. The concepts differ in what they draw and how you use them, not in colour.

Ranking and the pick: selection.md. Direction: creative-direction.md.

1. Soundings · ranked 1, chosen

A band of dials, then a watch list with a status column.

Idea
Every limit window is a ring: the arc is what's left, the tick is how much of the window's time is left. Arc past the tick means it will last. Agents are a list whose right-hand column holds the model and run time; it stays grey unless something needs you.
Solves
Nine lines of limits become one row you compare by eye. Short titles take one line. Sources become chips.
Refuses
Tiles and cards around gauges, a drawn value for a failed read, sentences for normal states.
You use it by
Looking. Selecting a dial opens that account's exact dates in place; a row opens its thread; the ended line expands.
Risk
Six or more windows wrap to a second row of dials.

2. Tide Table · ranked 3

One horizontal time axis with now in the middle.

Idea
The old Now Line, turned on its side. To the left of now, each agent is a bar as long as it has been running. To the right, each limit is a bar that reaches to its reset, filled by what's left.
Solves
"How long" and "until when" become lengths. The rose line keeps its meaning.
Refuses
Lists, rings, a hero number.
You use it by
Reading along the axis; hovering a bar for the exact time.
Why not
Half the width is all a title gets, so titles truncate, which the bar forbids. A 5-hour window and a 7-day window can't share a readable scale. Lineage has nowhere to go.

3. Orbit · ranked 4

One large radial figure: every window a ring around the count.

Idea
Apple's Activity rings, for quota. The count sits in the middle; each limit window is a concentric ring, lowest outermost.
Solves
One memorable figure. It would make a strong icon.
Refuses
A table of limits.
You use it by
Matching ring colours to a legend.
Why not
The legend is the old table again, so the text isn't gone. Past four rings it stops reading. A failed read and an empty window look alike as bare tracks. It spends 240 px on a figure before the first agent.

4. Still Water · ranked 5

Only what needs you, large; everything else one line each.

Idea
The panel is an inbox of exceptions. When nothing needs you it is almost empty: that is the calm in the name.
Solves
The least text of all five on a good evening.
Refuses
Showing anything that is fine.
You use it by
Opening "3 running" or "5 limits" when you want the rest.
Why not
It hides the names of running agents behind a click, which is the Mizu failure the first bar set out to beat. Cards on a raised fill are the widget look the owner rejected. "Lowest 9%" quietly summarises windows that must not be merged.

5. Ledger · ranked 2

One list, one row anatomy: name, meter, value.

Idea
Apple Stocks for agents. A limit row is a name, a bar with the time tick and a percent; an agent row is a title, a silence meter and a run time.
Solves
Every window keeps its date in view, and the meters make the column comparable. Nothing needs a click.
Refuses
Two-dimensional layouts.
You use it by
Scanning down two columns.
Why not, and what I keep
It is still one line per window, so it saves no height, and it is the "rows of text" the critique named, with a bar added. I keep its right-aligned value column and its tick-on-a-meter in the chosen concept: the tick moves onto the ring, the column onto the agent rows.
